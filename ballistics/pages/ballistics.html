Location, altitude, direction of fire

Latitude and direction of fire are only used for Coriolis; altitude turns the weather service's sea-level pressure into station pressure.

Pressure is station pressure (Kestrel, phone barometer), not the sea-level value weather services give.

Tinted rows are transonic (Mach 1.0–1.2), muted rows subsonic: drag data is least reliable there.

Velocity is measured by the chrono, so truing fits the drag only.

Log the elevation from your zero that centred a group, with that day's conditions. A group of 3–5, read from the group's centre, beats single shots. Shots still above Mach 1.2 true the velocity; shots further out true the drag.

Corrections

A load that hits to one side at the zero and can't be zeroed out (iron sights, or a scope zeroed for another load): a fixed angle, so the cm grow with range. It is added to the windage everywhere.

Spin drift needs the twist and the bullet's length. The formula is made for supersonic bullets and reads high for .22 LR: × about 0.6 matches Strelok. Empty = 1.

Bullet

A BC only means something with its drag model: use the G7 value for long boat-tail bullets, G1 when that is all the maker gives. Length is only needed for spin drift and stability.
